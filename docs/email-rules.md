# Email Rules

Tired of your inbox being a jumble of newsletters, bills, receipts, and
personal messages? Email rules (also called filters) sort incoming mail
automatically — no code, no apps to install, nothing to buy. The rules live in
your email provider and run on their servers, which means they work even when
your computer is off.

## What email rules can do

- File newsletters into a `Reading` folder so your inbox stays for people
- Flag bills and receipts so you never miss a due date
- Mark shopping receipts as read and archive them
- Move automated alerts (banking, shipping, pharmacy) to their own folders
- Keep spam-like senders out of your inbox without blocking them entirely

## Privacy note

Email rules run inside your email account — your mail stays with your provider.
Don't create rules that forward your email to an outside address unless you
trust that destination, and never paste your password into a "rule builder" on
a random website. Use the settings inside Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail, or
whatever provider you already use.

## Gmail (filters)

1. Open Gmail in a browser and click the **Show search options** icon
   (the slider icon) inside the search bar at the top.
2. Fill in what you want to match. Common examples:

   - **From:** `newsletter@` — catches newsletters
   - **Subject:** `invoice` or `receipt` — catches bills
   - **Has the words:** `unsubscribe` — catches marketing mail

3. Click **Create filter**, then choose what happens to matching mail:

   - **Apply the label:** create a label like `Reading`, `Bills`, or `Receipts`
   - **Also apply filter to matching conversations** — cleans up old mail too
   - **Skip the Inbox (Archive it)** — keeps your inbox for people
   - **Mark as read** — for receipts you just want filed

4. Click **Create filter** to save it.

Example: bills that never get lost
- From: `billing@` OR Subject: `invoice`
- Action: Apply label `Bills`, mark as important

Example: quiet reading folder
- From: contains `newsletter`
- Action: Apply label `Reading`, Skip the Inbox, mark as read

To edit or delete a filter later: Gmail → Settings (gear) → See all settings →
Filters and Blocked Addresses.

## Outlook / Outlook.com / Microsoft 365

1. Open Outlook and go to Settings → Mail → Rules.
2. Click **Add new rule**.
3. Give it a name (e.g. "File newsletters").
4. **Add a condition:** who it's from, what the subject contains, or words in
   the message.
5. **Add an action:** Move to, mark as, categorize, or delete.
6. Optionally **Add another action** (e.g. move to `Reading` AND mark as read).
7. Check **Run rule now** if you want it to clean up existing mail, then save.

To manage rules later: Settings → Mail → Rules. You can turn rules off, edit
them, or change their order (rules run top to bottom).

---

## Apple Mail (Mac)

1. Open Mail → Settings (or Preferences) → Rules.
2. Click **Add Rule**.
3. Give it a name.
4. Set conditions: any of / all of — from, subject, etc.
5. Set actions: Move mailbox to, set color, play sound, etc.
6. Click OK, then choose whether to apply it to existing mail in your folders.

Rules apply to new mail as it arrives. To apply to old mail, select messages,
then right-click → Apply Rules.

## Example rules to try today

| Goal | Condition | Action |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Tame newsletters | From contains `newsletter` | Apply label `Reading`, Skip Inbox |
| Never miss a bill | Subject contains `invoice` or `bill` | Apply label `Bills`, mark important |
| File receipts | From contains `receipt` | Apply label `Receipts`, mark as read |
| Shipping updates | Subject contains `tracking` or `delivery` | Apply label `Shipping` |
| Quiet the alerts | From contains `no-reply` | Apply label `Alerts`, mark as read |

## Troubleshooting

- **Rule isn't running:** Check that the rule is enabled/toggled on, and that
  you're looking at the right account (some providers have rules per account).
- **Old mail isn't sorted:** Most providers only apply new rules to new mail.
  Look for an option like "Apply to existing conversations" or run it manually.
- **Too many rules:** Start with 2–3 rules. Add more once you're comfortable.
  Rules stack, and order matters in Outlook.

Email rules are an organizational tool, not financial or medical advice. Your
email stays with your provider — nothing in this guide uploads your mail
anywhere.
