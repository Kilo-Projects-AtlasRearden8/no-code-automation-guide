Before you automate anything

This page takes two minutes to read and will save you from headaches later.

The golden rules

  1. Test on copies, never move originals. Every script in this manual works on a copy of your files by default. Run it on a test folder first. Only point it at your real files once you’re sure it does what you expect.
  2. Scripts preview before they act. Every script defaults to dry-run mode: it shows you what it would do without changing anything. You opt in to making changes (with --apply), never by accident.
  3. You can read every script. These are tiny Python scripts (under 100 lines each). Open them, read them, and only run them when you understand what they do.
  4. Start small. Automate one folder, one task, one afternoon. Don’t try to organize your whole computer in one sitting.
  5. Nothing leaves your computer. No script uploads your files, email, or calendar data. The only email that ever goes out is one you choose to send yourself, using your own settings.

Why we test on copies

If a script has a bug, you want it to scramble a test folder, not your only copy of last year’s tax documents. A copy costs nothing; your originals are irreplaceable.

How to make a test copy

  • Windows: copy your folder (e.g. Downloads) to Downloads Test.
  • Mac: right-click the folder → Duplicate.
  • Linux: cp -r Downloads Downloads-Test.

Point the script at the test copy first. Check the results. Then decide whether to run it on the real folder.

What these scripts do NOT do

  • They do not send your files anywhere. Everything stays on your computer.
  • They do not require API keys, accounts, or personal information.
  • They do not delete anything unless you explicitly opt in.
  • No credentials or secrets are stored in this project.
  • Calendar reminders and app connections stay local; nothing is posted to the web.
Remember: If a script ever asks for something that makes you uncomfortable, stop and read the source. You’re in control.
